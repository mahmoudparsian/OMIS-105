Here's the core of how I'd approach it:

**Week 1** builds confidence. Lecture 1 opens with "spreadsheets break — databases don't" and ends with every student running their first `SELECT *`. Lecture 2 teaches schema design through a bookstore exercise — they design their first table from a business description.

**Week 2** introduces relational thinking. Lecture 3 starts with a deliberately bad flat table and lets students discover the redundancy/anomaly problems themselves — that motivates splitting into multiple tables with foreign keys. Lecture 4 teaches JOINs as the tool that reconnects those tables to answer real questions.

**Week 3** unlocks reporting power. Lecture 5 covers GROUP BY and HAVING through the lens of "your VP asks for a department salary summary." Lecture 6 adds subqueries, then runs a comprehensive live challenge that ties all three weeks together.

The consistent pattern across all 6 lectures: start with a business question they care about, show why current tools can't answer it, introduce the SQL concept that can, then hands-on practice immediately. Every homework builds on the previous one, and Homework 6 is a mini-project that requires everything they've learned.
